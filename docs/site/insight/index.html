    What happens to your answer

    1. It is recorded as a lead, not as a fact. Everything from a person enters the same way, including from people who are certainly right. What you said and how you knew it are stored together and never separated.
    2. It is graded on two axes, how reliable the source is and how credible the content is, the way an intelligence report is. Nothing is thrown away for scoring low.
    3. It contributes a direction, not a number. A lead tells us where to look and what to rule out. The figure itself still has to come from the record, which is what stops anyone's recollection becoming a reported cost.
    4. You are told when it closes something. If your answer closes a question or opens a better one, you hear about it. Contributions are attributed to you internally and never published with your name without asking.

    Why your answer matters

    1. One answer can move a large block. The brief for each question shows the exposure the register records for it and the transactions behind it.
    2. Your name stays with your answer. Internally, so the answer can be traced back to how it was known; never published without asking.
    3. A pointer is enough. "It is in chapter 4 of the manual" or "ask the budget office" closes as many questions as a full answer.
    4. We will not ask twice. An answer is recorded once with its source.
